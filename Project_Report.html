1. Executive Summary

Inventory mismanagement represents one of the largest drains on profitability in retail enterprises. Traditional static replenishment heuristics (e.g., ordering static batches on fixed calendar days) frequently lead to either stockouts (lost revenue, dissatisfied customers) or overstocking (dead capital, inflated warehousing costs, product obsolescence).

This project designs and implements an end-to-end, production-grade Optimised Retail Inventory Management System. The system models 2 years of daily sales transactions across 20 retail products and 3 distinct categories (Electronics, Groceries, Apparel). Using machine learning (Random Forest Regressor) with lag features and calendar seasonality, the system forecasts future daily demand with a 54.6% reduction in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) compared to classical moving-average baselines. Forecasted demand is dynamically integrated into classical inventory control formulas—Safety Stock (SS), Reorder Point (ROP), and Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)—and presented through an interactive, multi-tab Streamlit executive dashboard.

2. Problem Statement & System Objectives

2.1 The Retail Dilemma

  • Understocking (Stockouts): Inability to meet customer demand during surges, leading to lost immediate sales and brand erosion.
  • Overstocking: Excessive inventory tied up in warehouse space, incurring annual holding costs (typically 15%–25% of unit cost per year), risk of theft/damage, and liquidity bottlenecks.
  • Seasonality & Promotion Blindness: Traditional heuristic systems fail to anticipate weekend demand boosts, holiday shopping surges (Q4), and discount campaign lifts.

2.2 Core Objectives

  1. Simulate realistic retail sales data incorporating weekly seasonality, holiday surges, dynamic pricing, and promotions.
  2. Train and validate an ML model to forecast demand using time-based evaluation splits with zero data leakage.
  3. Translate probabilistic demand into operational parameters: Safety Stock, Reorder Point, and EOQ.
  4. Provide store managers with an interactive decision cockpit with color-coded alerts and one-click purchase order CSV export.

4. Inventory Optimization Mathematical Formulations

4.1 Safety Stock (SS)

Safety stock acts as a protective buffer against demand spikes and vendor fulfillment delays during lead time:

SS = Z × √L × σd

Where:
• Z = 1.65 (Standard normal distribution score for 95% cycle service level)
• L = Supplier lead time in days (lead_time_days)
• σd = Standard deviation of daily demand

4.2 Reorder Point (ROP)

The inventory threshold that triggers replenishment so stock does not deplete to zero before delivery:

ROP = (d̄ × L) + SS

Where:
• d̄ = Average forecasted daily demand over the planning horizon
• L = Supplier lead time in days
• SS = Safety Stock

4.3 Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)

Balances fixed order placement expenses against annual warehouse inventory holding costs:

EOQ = √[ (2 × D × S) / H ]

Where:
• D = Projected Annual Demand = d̄ × 365
• S = Fixed administrative cost per order (ordering_cost)
• H = Unit annual carrying/holding cost (holding_cost)
